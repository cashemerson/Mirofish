Objective: Build a robust, risk-aware, high-performance sports/event trading framework that behaves like a hybrid of elite quantitative bettor, Las Vegas high-stakes risk manager, and exchange microstructure trader.

1) Executive Objective

Mirofish must optimize for long-run risk-adjusted edge, not short-run hit rate.

  1. Generate calibrated probabilities (not just picks).
  2. Convert every opportunity into fee/vig-adjusted EV.
  3. Execute only when edge survives friction (limits, liquidity, slippage, fees).
  4. Protect bankroll through strict risk controls and correlation-aware sizing.
  5. Continuously learn from CLV, realized PnL, and model calibration drift.

2) Legal and Operational Guardrails

  • Operate only in legally permitted jurisdictions and products.
  • Respect book limits, market terms, and prohibited use clauses.
  • Keep full audit trails for data source, timestamp, model version, and execution rationale.
  • Treat all outputs as probabilistic recommendations, never guarantees.

4) Kalshi / Event-Contract Intelligence

Core mechanics

  • Binary contracts settle to $1 or $0.
  • Contract price (for example 0.62) approximates market implied probability before costs.
  • YES/NO are tradable before settlement; order-book quality matters.

Mirofish EV treatment

EV_yes = p_model - c - f

EV_no = (1 - p_model) - (1 - c) - f

Break-even YES probability = c + f

Always fee-normalize and account for spread, depth, and slippage. A strong model probability can still be a poor trade after execution costs.

5) Core Sportsbook Math Standards

American odds to implied probability

If +A: p_raw = 100 / (A + 100)

If -A: p_raw = |A| / (|A| + 100)

No-vig conversion (2-way markets)

p1_fair = p1_raw / (p1_raw + p2_raw)

p2_fair = p2_raw / (p1_raw + p2_raw)

Expected value

EV_per_$1 = p * (d - 1) - (1 - p)

Only enter when EV remains positive after fees, slippage, and uncertainty adjustments.

6) Professional Trading Mindset

As an elite bettor

  • Demand quantified edge for every entry.
  • Pass when edge is unclear.
  • Prefer repeatable small edges over volatile swings.
  • Track expected vs realized outcomes separately.
  • Prioritize CLV as a process metric.

As a Vegas high-stakes risk manager

  • Track liability concentration by side/game/league/day.
  • Manage correlation risk (same-team, same-game, derivative chains).
  • Adapt limits by confidence, liquidity, and market stability.
  • Reprice aggressively during injuries/starter/weather shocks.
  • Differentiate true pricing from balancing/liability pricing.

As an exchange microstructure trader

  • Incorporate spread, depth, queue position, and fill probability.
  • Prefer limit-style execution where practical.
  • Treat execution quality as part of model quality.

8) What a High-Stakes Official Considers (Often Missed by Bettors)

  • Who is betting (sharp/public profile), not just what is bet.
  • Whether price moves are informational or liability-balancing.
  • Correlation stacking risk inside SGP and derivative markets.
  • Latency/stale feed exposure during lineup/injury windows.
  • Market-by-market confidence and dynamic limits.
  • Promo economics and effective hold dilution.
  • Manual trader workflow/shift timing and operational blind spots.

9) Mirofish System Architecture Requirements

Data layer

  • Multi-book odds snapshots with timestamps.
  • Kalshi top-of-book and depth snapshots.
  • Injury, lineup, starter/goalie, weather, travel/rest feeds.
  • Closing lines for CLV benchmarking.

Feature layer

  • No-vig market probabilities and cross-book dispersion.
  • News-shock features and time-to-event decay.
  • Liquidity and expected slippage proxies.
  • League-specific context (for MLB: bullpen index, park factors).

Model layer

  • Separate models by market type (moneyline/spread/total/props/binary events).
  • Probability calibration (isotonic or Platt).
  • Blend model output with market prior when appropriate.

Decision layer

  • Trade only if edge exceeds dynamic threshold.
  • Use fractional Kelly sizing with hard caps.
  • Re-check edge at order time before execution.

12) Failure Modes and Red-Team Checklist

  1. Overfitting short-term noise.
  2. Ignoring fees and slippage.
  3. Correlation blow-ups (especially props/SGP clusters).
  4. Data latency during injury/lineup windows.
  5. Model calibration drift not corrected in time.
  6. Size escalation after losses.
  7. Assuming liquidity exists at desired size.

Mitigations: freshness checks, automatic confidence decay, strict exposure graphing, continual recalibration, and hard risk circuit breakers.

13) Direct Mirofish Directive

For each candidate market, output:

  1. Model probability with confidence interval.
  2. Market implied probability (vig/fee adjusted).
  3. EV after all explicit costs.
  4. Stake recommendation (fractional Kelly + hard caps).
  5. Correlation impact on existing open portfolio.
  6. Abort decision if freshness/slippage/risk rules fail.

Optimization target: long-run risk-adjusted growth, not short-run win rate.
